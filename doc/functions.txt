Working document -- Clojure seq functions <-> musical operations
(sequable-domain / sq exploration, 2026-08-07)

Not yet implemented as real functions -- brainstorming for what
transforms would be worth building on top of sq once it's decided
whether that line of work is kept. See doc/domain.md and CLAUDE.md
for the actual shipped API; this file is scratch, not reference.

================================================================
1. Clojure seq fn -> closest musical equivalent
================================================================

Clojure fn         | Example                            | Musical equivalent                              | Why
--------------------|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|----
map                | (map (transpose 7) notes)          | Transpose                                        | 1:1 transform applied to every note -- pitch shift is the canonical case, but any per-note remap (dynamic, articulation) fits the same shape
map (2 colls)      | (map harmonize melody interval-line)| Harmonization                                    | zipping two streams together note-for-note -- pairing each melody note with a harmony tone
filter             | (filter downbeat? notes)           | Thinning / voice reduction                       | keep only notes matching a predicate -- extracting the strong beats, or notes above a register
remove             | (remove pp? notes)                 | Muting / silencing                               | drop notes matching a predicate -- the inverse of filter
reverse            | (reverse phrase)                   | Retrograde                                       | exact match to the classical/serialist term -- playing material backward. This codebase already has this exact idea: context.clj's env-reverse
cycle              | (cycle riff)                       | Ostinato / vamp                                  | a pattern repeated indefinitely
repeat             | (repeat n)                         | Pedal tone / drone                               | one value repeated indefinitely -- a sustained note rather than a pattern
iterate            | (iterate (transpose 2) motif)      | Sequence (the real music-theory term)            | a motif repeated at successively shifted pitch levels -- literally the classical definition of a melodic "sequence"
take               | (take 8 phrase)                    | Excerpt / truncation                             | cutting a phrase short
drop               | (drop 1 phrase)                    | Anacrusis removal                                | skipping a pickup/lead-in
take-while         | (take-while (complement rest?) notes) | Phrase-until-breath                           | play until some condition breaks (a rest, a range limit)
interleave         | (interleave voice-a voice-b)       | Hocket                                           | alternating single notes/groups between voices to form one composite line -- a real medieval technique, exact match
interpose          | (interpose click notes)            | Click-track insertion / grace notes              | inserting a fixed element between every pair -- a metronome click, or an ornament between each note
partition / partition-all | (partition 4 notes)         | Beaming / bar grouping                           | chunking a flat stream into beat- or bar-groups -- mirrors this codebase's own Meter subdivisions
shuffle            | (shuffle notes)                    | Aleatoric reordering                             | chance-based ordering, a la Cage
sort-by            | (sort-by :pitches chord)           | Arpeggiation / voicing                           | turning a simultaneous chord into an ordered run -- the "chord -> arpeggio" case that came up with sq+:PAR
distinct           | (distinct pitches)                 | Pitch-class / row extraction                     | the unique pitch collection in use -- deriving a scale or tone row
mapcat             | (mapcat expand-trill notes)        | Ornament expansion                               | one note becomes several -- literally what core.domain.ornaments already does
take-nth           | (take-nth 4 notes)                 | Downbeat extraction                              | every Nth note -- pulling out the metric skeleton
frequencies        | (frequencies pitches)              | Pitch histogram / tonal-center analysis          | counting occurrences to infer a key center
reductions         | (reductions + intervals)           | Walking line                                     | like reduce but keeps every intermediate step -- a walking bass built interval by interval
group-by           | (group-by register notes)          | Voice separation                                 | splitting one stream into several by some criterion (register, channel)
rand-nth           | (rand-nth scale)                   | Improvised choice                                | picking a note at random from a set
flatten            | (flatten nested-pars)              | Homophonic reduction                             | collapsing nested grouping into one flat stream
concat             | (concat verse chorus)              | Medley / splice                                  | one after another -- what :SEQ already means structurally

A few of these (retrograde/reverse, ornament expansion/mapcat,
arpeggiation/sort-by) aren't just analogies -- they line up with
mechanisms already in the codebase (env-reverse, core.domain.ornaments,
the :PAR-cycled-to-sequential default from the sq work).

================================================================
2. Position map -- functions used WITHIN play's argument vs. as a
   WRAPPER around play/display's result
================================================================

Worth separating out precisely, because the reason is mechanical, not
incidental: play's *argument* goes through its own custom dispatch
(play-form) -- that's a contract only certain shapes satisfy, and today
only keyword?/vector? do (which is the whole reason the sq work exists
-- to widen that contract). play's *return value*, once you're past
that dispatch, is just an ordinary Clojure value -- nothing special
about it, so any function applies to it freely, no widening needed.

One correction to the premise first: (cycle (play :part)) "works" only
in the trivial sense of not erroring -- play returns nil (fire-and-
forget, it triggers async playback and gets out of the way), and
(cycle nil) is just (). There's nothing there to cycle over. The
genuinely useful version of that pattern is wrapping display, not play
-- display actually returns a real vector of resolved steps, so post-
processing it is meaningful.

Position                          | What it operates on                                        | Needs                                                                                | Examples
------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------
Inside play's argument -- reshaping the *material* before it sounds | domain values (leaves/containers pulled out via sq) | the material-side contract: sq to get a real seq, and play-form widened to accept whatever comes back out | (play (take 4 (cycle (sq :part)))), (play (map (transpose 7) (sq :part))), (play (interleave (sq :a) (sq :b))), (play (filter downbeat? (sq :part)))
Wrapping display's result -- inspecting/post-processing what *would* sound | resolved MidiEvent maps (:onset/:velocity/:pitches/:dur-secs/...) | nothing special -- display already returns a plain vector | (take 4 (display :part)) (peek at just the first 4 resolved events), (frequencies (map :pitches (display :part))), (filter #(> (:velocity %) 100) (display :part))
Wrapping play's result -- degenerate, not a real pattern | nil | -- | (cycle (play :part)) => (), always. Doesn't error, does nothing. Don't build on this one.

Practical rule: if you're *shaping what plays*, the seq function goes
on the inside, feeding sq's output to play. If you're *inspecting what
would play*, the seq function goes on the outside, wrapping display's
output. They're operating on two different kinds of data -- domain
parts vs. resolved events -- not the same operation happening on either
side of a symmetrical boundary.
