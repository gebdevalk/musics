Simplicity audit — musics
===========================
2026-09-17. Scope: is the system too complicated, and where specifically.
Method: direct file/line counts, grep-based reachability checks (what's
required from where), and comparison against the project's OWN prior
self-audits (audit-summary.txt, audit-wall-mechanism.txt, audit-algo-tree.txt,
audit-gui-manipulability.txt, all dated 2026-09-10) to see what's changed
since and whether prior recommendations landed. Not a re-run of those —
this picks up where they left off and adds what they didn't cover
(CLAUDE.md size, repo-root doc clutter, grammar residue, doc/algorithms.md
staleness).

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-------------------
- The single biggest, cheapest lever: algo/ has grown to 39 files / 6958
  lines, and 23 of those 39 files (59%) have ZERO references from
  anywhere outside algo/ itself — not wired into core.wall, play, the
  grammar, or musics.core. This isn't new (a 2026-09-10 audit already
  flagged it and recommended a one-table index), but 2,277 MORE lines
  of unwired general-purpose machinery (algo.toolkit, algo.algoline,
  algo.dimensions) were added on 2026-09-12/14, AFTER that audit, still
  with zero engine wiring — the trend is toward more of this, not less.
- doc/algorithms.md (the project's own "what's actually wired" table,
  last touched 2026-09-15) does not mention toolkit/algoline/dimensions
  at all, despite them being the three largest files in algo/. The
  project's own map of itself is already out of date.
- CLAUDE.md is 1706 lines. 228 of those lines are pure "Wave 1...7"
  historical narration that the project's own stated documentation
  convention says belongs in doc/decisions.md, condensed — and says so
  explicitly, while also saying it won't retroactively clean this up.
  Nobody has come back to do it.
- The repo root has 11 untracked audit/analysis .txt files (3151
  lines total: review.txt, algo.txt, algo-matrix.txt, algo-composition.txt,
  algo-stages.txt, audit-*.txt x4, indispensability-adherence.txt,
  assist.txt) with no index and no indication which are current vs.
  superseded by a later one on the same topic.
- The core mechanism itself (core.repo / core.conductor / core.wall /
  core.compose / core.async-engine, ~11 global registries in
  core.registries.clj) is NOT bloated relative to what it does — this
  echoes the 2026-09-10 audit's own conclusion ("not too complex, just
  not visible") and nothing found here overturns that. Don't spend
  effort shrinking this; spend it on visibility (see below) and on the
  algo/ mass, which IS new, unreviewed growth.
- The grammar (musics.ebnf) is a genuine positive finding: despite ~7
  rounds of bracket-scheme churn per project memory, no dead rules or
  residue from earlier schemes were found on inspection. Each rewrite
  replaced cleanly rather than accumulating. Not a simplification
  target.
- Small, cheap wins found and not yet acted on from the 2026-09-10
  audit: alias-pair surface area (bld!/build!, reg-factory!/
  register-factory!, unreg-factory!/unregister-factory!) still present
  in musics/core.clj, still roughly doubling name count for zero added
  concepts.

FINDING 1 — algo/ unwired mass, and it's GROWING, not shrinking
======================================================================
Kind: accidental/accretion complexity. Impact: HIGH. Risk of fixing: LOW
(deleting/archiving unreferenced code cannot break anything that
currently works, by definition of "unreferenced").

Evidence:
  find src/algo -name '*.clj' | wc -l        -> 39 files
  find src/algo -name '*.clj' | xargs wc -l  -> 6958 lines total

  Per-file "is this namespace required by anything outside algo/":
    23 of 39 files: 0 outside references
     9 of 39 files: 1 outside reference
     4 of 39 files: 2
     2 of 39 files: 3
     1 of 39 files: 4 (algo.random — the one genuinely load-bearing file,
                        required by musics/core.clj itself)

  The entire algo/rhythmic/ subdirectory (13 files, 1398 lines —
  phase_sieve, poly, necklace, stochastic, physical, transform,
  sonification, constraint, fractal_geometric, world, decompose,
  constraint, micro) has ZERO references from outside algo/. This
  matches the 2026-09-10 audit's finding ("ALL 13 files in rhythmic/,
  the biggest subdirectory, have zero engine connection") — unchanged
  seven days later.

  NEW since that audit: three top-level files were added 2026-09-12
  through 2026-09-14 (confirmed via `git log --diff-filter=A`):
    src/algo/toolkit.clj     946 lines  (2 outside refs: both examples/)
    src/algo/algoline.clj    512 lines  (2 outside refs: both examples/)
    src/algo/dimensions.clj  819 lines  (0 outside refs)
  2,277 lines combined — a third of algo/'s total mass — and every one
  of them is, per its own docstrings, a general-purpose exploration
  framework ("composable interceptor" pipeline, a taxonomy over the
  whole algo/ tree, "general-purpose building blocks for algoline").
  Their only real callers are two files in src/examples/
  (cyclic_random_algoline.clj, indispensability_algoline.clj, 123 and
  180 lines) plus those two files' own tests. Nothing in core.wall,
  core.async-engine, or the grammar touches any of the three.

  Per project memory this work was explicitly "SHIPPED to main" as
  finished — it is not in-progress scaffolding, it's considered done,
  and it still doesn't connect to the actual playback engine.

  doc/algorithms.md (the project's own canonical "LIVE vs STATIC, wired
  vs not" reference table, last edited 2026-09-15 — one day after
  dimensions.clj) does not mention toolkit.clj, algoline.clj, or
  dimensions.clj anywhere. The table that exists specifically to answer
  "is this wired" is already silent on the three newest, largest files.

Why this is complexity, not design: none of this is wrong code — it's
tested (test/toolkit_test.clj, test/algoline_test.clj,
test/algo_dimensions_test.clj, test/cyclic_random_algoline_test.clj,
test/indispensability_algoline_test.clj all exist and presumably pass).
But it is mass a reader has to wade through, decide is or isn't relevant,
and mentally file away, for zero current playback capability. The
2026-09-10 audit already named this dynamic exactly: "not too complex,
just not visible" — and the response since then was to add more
unwired material rather than wire the existing material in or clearly
segregate it.

Suggestions (pick based on appetite):
  a) Cheapest, lowest-risk: move src/algo/rhythmic/*, algo.toolkit,
     algo.algoline, algo.dimensions (and anything else at 0 outside
     refs) into a clearly-labeled src/algo/exploratory/ or
     src/sketchbook/ directory, OR add one line to each file's ns
     docstring: "not wired into core.wall/play; static/exploratory
     only." Either makes "is this live" answerable by directory
     listing alone, not by running the grep this audit ran. ~1 hour.
  b) Do the 2026-09-10 audit's own recommended step 1: update
     doc/algorithms.md's table to include toolkit/algoline/dimensions
     (mark them STATIC/exploratory, same as the rest of rhythmic/).
     ~30 min, and closes the immediate staleness gap.
  c) Higher-effort, higher-payoff: pick ONE of the unwired
     rhythmic/ generators (already flagged in the Sept-10 audit as the
     natural next step) and actually wire it as a core.wall factory,
     as a single worked, committed example — this was recommendation
     #2 in audit-summary.txt and still hasn't happened.
  d) If genuinely dead (not "future material," just abandoned
     exploration): delete algo.toolkit/algoline/dimensions and
     src/examples/ outright. This is the biggest line-count win
     (2,277 + 303 = 2,580 lines) but is a judgment call only the user
     can make about whether the algoline exploration is still wanted.

they're meant as scratch/working files, not project history worth
keeping — but that contradicts them clearly being written to inform
future sessions (this one included, via project memory pointing at
them).

FINDING 3 — CLAUDE.md: 1706 lines, 228 of them pure history
======================================================================
Kind: documentation/cognitive complexity. Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH (this is
the file every session — human or Claude — reads first to get
oriented). Risk: LOW (moving prose to a different file, not deleting
information).

Evidence: `wc -l CLAUDE.md` -> 1706 lines total. The "## Repo state —
read this first" section (Waves 1 through 7, git-log-style narration of
how the system got to its current shape) runs 228 lines
(`awk '/^## Repo state/,/^## Commands/'`), roughly 13% of the whole
file, sitting between the top-level intro and the "## Commands" section
every reader needs early.

The file's OWN stated convention (the "## Documentation conventions"
section, a few lines above "Repo state") says explicitly:
  "New or edited documentation... should describe the current system,
  not narrate how it got there... The reasoning behind a design choice
  goes in doc/decisions.md instead... This is a going-forward
  convention, not a retroactive one: the 'Repo state' section right
  below... [is] not being rewritten under this policy on their own —
  they get brought in line with it if and when whatever they describe
  is next touched anyway, not as a dedicated pass."

So this is a known, named debt, not a surprise — but "if and when next
touched" has apparently not triggered in the many changes since Wave 7
was written (chordmode, GUI adviser-highlight, algoline/toolkit/
dimensions all postdate Wave 7 and none prompted a pass). The 700+ lines
the user's own impression cited is roughly right for the historical
portion once you count the "Wave" prose plus the similarly-narrated
asides scattered through later sections (Session/repo, Conductor, Wall
— each has its own "used to be X, this session changed it to Y"
paragraphs, per the same stated exemption).

Why this matters even though it's "just docs": CLAUDE.md is the first
and often only thing read to build a mental model of this system
(explicitly by Claude Code sessions, and per its own header, intended
for human maintainers too). A 1706-line front door is a real cost paid
on every single session/onboarding, independent of whether the
underlying code is simple or not.

Suggestion: do the dedicated pass the file's own convention defers.
Concretely: extract "Wave 1" through "Wave 7" (the "## Repo state"
section) into doc/decisions.md as dated, condensed entries (the format
decisions.md already uses elsewhere), and replace the section in
CLAUDE.md with a 1-paragraph summary of current state only ("the flat,
versioned-repo model is in place; see doc/decisions.md for the history
of how it got there"). Do the same for the smaller narrated asides
inside "Session, the versioned repo, and playback," "Conductor," and
"Wall" once those are touched next for unrelated reasons, per the
project's own stated policy — no need to force all of it in one pass.
Rough effort for just the "Repo state" section: 2-3 hours (careful
reading + condensing, not mechanical). This is pure documentation
surgery — zero code risk — but it's real editorial work, not a
five-minute delete.

FINDING 4 — registry/mechanism count: confirmed real, but NOT new
======================================================================
Kind: essential complexity (mostly) with a documentation-visibility gap.
Impact: LOW as a simplification target (little here should be merged or
removed) — MEDIUM as a "write it down in one place" fix.

Evidence: `grep -n "^(defonce" src/core/registries.clj` finds 11
distinct global mutable stores in one file alone:
  1. repo registry (id -> tx -> node)           — core.repo
  2. staging sets (in-progress tx edits)        — core.repo
  3. tx counter                                  — core.repo
  4. staging-id counter                          — core.repo
  5. algo-factory-registry (permanent recipes)  — core.wall
  6. algo-registry (built, hot-swappable)       — core.wall
  7. criterion registry                          — core.wall
  8. distribution registry                       — core.wall
  9. action-registry (parked toolbox)            — core.conductor
  10. schedule table (one-shot)                  — core.conductor
  11. repeating-schedule table                   — core.conductor
Plus a 12th outside that file: algo-assignments / :algo-prepared
(src/core/async_engine.clj:1075/1137), and a 13th concept,
configure-preset!'s wall-preset store (per project memory, DONE
2026-08-30, "second registry, independent config per preset").

Assessment: this is real, and it is a lot to hold in your head — but
per CLAUDE.md's own explicit reasoning (referencing review.txt point 1)
this is a *stated, deliberate* tradeoff for a single-user REPL tool
where global mutable atoms are simpler than threading state explicitly
everywhere, not an oversight. Each store also does genuinely differ
from its neighbors when you look closely (factory-vs-built-algo split
exists specifically to prevent a confirmed real bug; one-shot vs.
repeating schedule tables are genuinely different semantics) — this
echoes the 2026-09-10 wall-mechanism audit's own conclusion under
"GENUINELY-NECESSARY, PULLS ITS WEIGHT."

This audit does NOT recommend merging or removing any of these 13 — do
not treat this finding as a simplification target on its own. It's
listed here only because the user's stated impression ("too
complicated") likely traces partly to this count, and the honest answer
is: mostly justified, but never inventoried in one place a reader can
scan. No single file or doc section lists "here are all N stores, in
one table, with a one-line purpose each" — a reader has to walk
core.registries.clj's docstrings one at a time to reconstruct this list
themselves (which is exactly what this audit did).

Suggestion: add one small table (10-15 lines) to core.registries.clj's
own ns docstring, or to CLAUDE.md near "Repo state" once that section
is condensed per Finding 3 — name, purpose, one line each. Cheap
(~30 min), directly answers "how many moving pieces are there" without
requiring re-deriving it by reading 11 docstrings.

FINDING 5 — still-present alias-pair surface area (known, unfixed)
======================================================================
Kind: accidental complexity. Impact: LOW. Risk: LOW (pure renames/
removals with a handful of call sites each).

Evidence: the 2026-09-10 wall-mechanism audit flagged "short-alias fn
pairs (build!/bld!, reg-factory!/register-factory!) roughly double the
name count without adding concepts." Confirmed still present, unchanged,
in src/musics/core.clj:
  line 1555: reg-factory!
  line 1567: unreg-factory!   (calls unregister-factory!)
  line 1612: bld!             (calls build!)
Three alias functions, each a trivial wrapper, each still requiring a
reader to learn "these are the same thing" rather than there being one
name. Not fixed since being named seven days ago.

Suggestion: pick one spelling per pair and delete the other (aliases
are cheap to grep-replace at call sites; check test/ and doc/ usage
first). If the short forms are kept deliberately for REPL-typing
convenience, at minimum say so once in a comment so a future audit
doesn't re-flag it as an oversight. ~30 min including a grep for
existing callers.

FINDING 6 — grammar (musics.ebnf): no residue found — NOT a target
======================================================================
Kind: n/a — explicitly checked and ruled out, noted so it isn't
re-investigated needlessly.

Given ~7 rounds of bracket-scheme churn (per project memory: `#{}` ->
`(par ...)`, `\keyword`-prefixed commands dropped, `@[ ]`/`@{ }` added
then fully removed), the expectation going in was to find dead grammar
rules or walker cases for now-unreachable syntax. Direct inspection of
src/input/musics.ebnf (646 lines) found none: every rule enumerated
(TopElement, BarLine, chordmode/ChordModeNote/ChordAddition/ChordBass,
grace, VarDef/VarRef/VarName, Partial, KeyAssignment/AssignName,
TempoMark, PitchLetterAbs/Rel, Ornament/Modifier, SlurMark/Tie/Chord/
Rest/MultiRest, Reference, Comment, etc.) corresponds to real, current
syntax with no obsolete leftovers spotted. Each historical rewrite
appears to have been a clean replacement, not an addition on top of the
old shape. This matches CLAUDE.md's own framing of Waves 6/7 as full
project-wide migrations (every real .mus file, test suite, and importer
updated in the same pass), not partial ones.

No action needed here — flagged so this audit's reader doesn't spend
effort double-checking a place that already turned out clean.

======================================================================
ESSENTIAL COMPLEXITY — explicitly NOT simplification targets
======================================================================
Listed so the user can see what was checked and deliberately excluded,
not just what wasn't checked.

- The three-tier separation (Material / Sound / The playground) —
  genuinely different responsibilities (durable parsed content vs.
  real-time dispatch vs. one-off performance choices), and CLAUDE.md is
  explicit that no protocol enforces the boundary — it's a naming/
  dependency-direction convention, not an abstraction layer you could
  "simplify away" without losing the distinction it documents.
- core.repo's versioned, tx-addressed store — solves a real, live
  problem (editing material without glitching a currently-sounding
  performance) that a simpler single-mutable-map design demonstrably
  couldn't (per Wave 2-4's own documented motivation, including a
  specific confirmed bug: one shared play-tx pointer conflated
  independent voices' cutover timing).
- Per-voice :tx / :algo / :bar / :clock (rather than one shared,
  engine-wide value each) — same category, fixes confirmed bugs where
  a shared pointer/counter caused one voice's change to glitch another.
- The registry count (Finding 4) — real but individually justified;
  see that finding's own assessment.
- core.compose's split from core.async-engine (2026-09-10) — verified
  in doc/decisions.md as deliberately checked function-by-function for
  zero engine dependency before the move, and explicitly reasoned
  through as NOT introducing a new architectural tier. Sound design,
  not accidental complexity.
